# C3RS event-label and text review


Purpose: inspect what supports the recorded labels and what callbacks add.


Training-preview records only; sampled among reports with candidate callbacks. This intentionally selected sample cannot estimate population label accuracy or the proportion of callbacks that are useful.


Text is retained in full and organized by original person slot. Different people may supply the narrative and callback in the same record.



## Example 1 | ACN 28213


**Category sampled:** Operating Cab / Car Event - Station Operations


**Date:** 2023-04


**Six study codes selected:** Operating Cab / Car Event - Station Operations


### Synopsis


An Assistant Conductor did not open the doors in the correct direction at a station stop, resulting in doors being opened off the platform.


### Narrative 1


Arrived at [the] Station on [the] Train, I was positioned in the third car, threw the switch believing to be in [the] Car, and opened doors in [the] wrong direction, which was off the platform. I quickly closed [the] doors. No injury occurred.


### Callback 1


The reporter, an Assistant Conductor, stated there was some confusion about the reporter's location in the train at the time of the incident, and the equipment type was different from what the reporter was used to working. The reporter and another Assistant Conductor had discussed during the Job Safety Briefing that whoever was closest to the car prior to arriving at the station stop would be responsible for opening the correct doors. The reporter, believing the reporter was actually in the designated car, opened the doors' in the wrong direction, stating that the doors panels were side-by-side, and were a bit confusing and unfamiliar. The reporter noted that losing place within the consist, unfamiliarity with the equipment, being rushed to get into the correct spot for the door panel, and being distracted with collecting fares all contributed to the incident; however, the doors were immediately closed with no further exceptions.


### Narrative 2


[The] Train arrived at [the] Station when [the] Assistant Conductor threw [the] switch in [the] third car, thinking he was in the second car, and opened doors in [the] wrong direction off [the] platform. He quickly closed the doors, and no injury occurred.


### Narrative 3


Upon arrival at [the] Station, [the] Assistant Conductor reported he was in the third car instead of the second car to operate doors at the station. [They] opened the rear four cars instead of the front two cars due to not being in the right car for the shorter platform.


### Narrative 4


It was brought to my attention from the Conductor that when [the] Train was platformed at [the] Station (two-car platform), the Assistant Conductor inadvertently opened the rear four cars that were not on the platform. When the Assistant Conductor realized, they immediately closed the doors. I did not observe the doors being opened on the Screen in the cab.

## Example 2 | ACN 23538


**Category sampled:** Operating Cab / Car Event - Station Operations


**Date:** 2021-09


**Six study codes selected:** Operating Cab / Car Event - Station Operations


### Synopsis


A Train Crew reported a passenger car door was opened off the platform at a station stop. Immediately after noticing, the open door was closed.


### Narrative 1


[The] train pulled a little too long at Station X. The northernmost car was off the platform, the Engineer hit the door override. The Trainman told the one passenger looking to exit to walk back to the next door. The Trainman was keyed out [on] door 1 of Car A. After the passenger walked back, the Trainman walked to door 3 of Car B and opened the doors so the passengers could exit. After all the passengers were off, the Trainman closed the doors and gave 2 to buzzers to go. The Engineer did not have a door light because door 1 in Car A was still open. The Engineer tried to take power but couldn't. The Trainman closed door 3 in Car B, and the Engineer was then able to take power. The Trainman observed the platform and then closed door 1 [on] Car C. I, the Conductor, was in the vestibule of Car D, which was the 5th car from the north end. We [had] a 7 car consist plus the engine. When we [left] Station Y, the Trainman cut the entire head car off using the through switch. I, the Conductor, cut the rear 2 cars off. We only opened the belly 4 cars, because it is only a 4 car platform. I noticed we were long. I heard the Engineer on the radio and I asked on the public address system how we looked on [the] north end. The Trainman said he would get the doors. At that point, he took over the doors for that station only.


### Callback 1


The reporter, a Conductor, stated the Trainman was in charge of the door operations, per the Job Safety Briefing. The open door was closed by the Trainman once the Engineer made the crew aware. The reporter mentioned that the Trainman walked away from the open door to assist with a passenger. Due to the through switch not being thrown, the door in question did not close when the doors were keyed, the Conductor stated. A second Job Safety Briefing was conducted with the Trainman, per the reporter.


### Narrative 2


I was the Engineer of [the] Train, a passenger train from Station Z to Station A. We were using 7 cars until Station Y and then [went] to 4 [cars]. The most northern car (Car A) [was] to be locked out, and we were going to utilize cars 2, 3, 4 [and] 5 for our 4 car platforms. At Station X, I received a 2 buzzer indication; it was okay to move the train north. I took power and released the brakes. I noticed that I did not have a door light. I cut the power, reapplied the brakes and radioed to the crew that I did not have a door light. After a few minutes, I got a door light and resumed travel north. The crew later informed me that someone had keyed out of an open door and walked back to let a passenger off at another door. After completing the station work, I was given 2 buzzers to go, but [the] door was still open. [The] crew corrected [the] issue, and we were delayed about 3 minutes.


### Narrative 3


While pulling into Station X, we pulled a bit north of the platform. The northernmost car was off the platform. [The] Engineer hit [the] door override [and] I told passengers looking to exit at the northern door to walk back to [the] next door. I was keyed out in door 1 of Car A. After passengers walked back, I walked to door 3 of Car B and opened doors so passengers could exit. After all passengers were off, I closed [the] doors and gave 2 buzzers to go. [The] Engineer did not have [a] door light because door 1 in Car A was still open. [The Engineer] tried to take power, but couldn't. I closed door 3 in Car B, and [the] Engineer was able to take power. We pulled north of Station X. I observed [the] platform and then closed door 1 of Car A.

## Example 4 | ACN 36083


**Category sampled:** Speed Deviation - Maximum Authorized Speed


**Date:** 2025-03


**Six study codes selected:** Speed Deviation - Maximum Authorized Speed; Braking Event - Emergency / Penalty Brake Application; Speed Deviation - Temporary Speed Restriction


### Synopsis


An Engineer reported operating overspeed in a Temporary Speed Restriction.


### Narrative 1


While operating Train A, I exceeded the published Temporary Speed Restriction (TSR) in effect for Interlocking X in both directions on all tracks, which was X MPH. After passing the Home Signal entering Interlocking X from [the] Tunnel, I operated the train at Y MPH [5 MPH overspeed] until reaching the 10-unit marker on Track X in Station X. I did not realize my mistake until after I had changed ends and saw the Track Bulletin I had clipped onto the timetable page. Before departing Station Y, I did complete a Job [Safety] Briefing with the Brakeman and mentioned the restriction. At the time of the incident, I was unusually hot headed because of a few things that happened enroute. While entering Station Z at speed, midway down the platform, a teenager suddenly made a furtive movement toward the platform edge, which alarmed me and caused a visceral reaction. I chose not to report it because the train did not come in contact with the person, and my Conductor and I have gotten negative attitudes from reporting previous incidents, either via telephone or radio. In addition, while entering Station A, I experienced radio problems identifying myself to [the] Local Dispatcher (we did not have the entire lineup into the station at that moment and hit a downward cab signal). There was also angry radio chatter between a Crew Member on another train and [the] Local Dispatcher regarding interpretation of the rules on proceeding without a Route Indication, which served to reinforce my own attitude at the time. Generally speaking, Train A is particularly demanding for me because despite my best efforts, the train loses time as we progress west, and especially when our scheduled connection at Station Y arrives late. There is a short turn at Station X, and we would be out of assignment if we arrived late (my Crew and I have a standing agreement to try and stay in assignment if our collective attention to safety allows). My attention on this day was particularly strained as we were operating with 10 cars when we normally operated with eight, which sometimes calls for stopping the train at another marker to platform the train properly. Just prior to entering [the] Tunnel, we encountered three downward cab signals causing a penalty brake application, of which one was my mistake, and the other two were due to the infrastructure. To prevent a recurrence, I will remind myself to hold the physical Track Bulletin in my hand while enroute and when possible, arrange to have a Crew Member at the head end with me to remind me to comply with the restriction.


### Callback 1


The reporter, an Engineer, stated being fatigued due to the workload. The reporter also reiterated feelings of anger, stress, being rushed, distracted and an adrenaline rush due to events that took place during the day. The reporter now clips the Track Bulletin to the timetable in order to view the upcoming Temporary Speed Restrictions (TSRs). The reporter also calls out to the Conductor any upcoming TSRs as a reminder to himself and if the Conductor doesn't hear from the reporter the reporter thinks maybe the Conductor should call out the TSRs.

[The] train was traveling at Maximum Authorized Speed of X MPH. PTC is more conservative and issued a warning without enough time to react before applying a penalty, according to the reporter. The Engineer attested that having the Student Conductor on the head end of the train caused a distraction.

**Category sampled:** Signal Events - On Board Control System


**Date:** 2026-03


**Six study codes selected:** Mechanical - Less Severe; Signal Events - On Board Control System


### Synopsis


An Engineer reported a train was delayed at the initial station, citing a Positive Train Control malfunction as a contributing factor.


### Narrative 1


Prior to departing Station X as Train A, I noticed [the] Automatic Train Control (ATC)/Positive Train Control (PTC) tests were due to expire while enroute. [I] notified [the] proper authority to have [the] paperwork updated prior to departure. Station X Mechanical said the paperwork was okay to depart at scheduled leaving time. The reporter mentioned that training did not cover this situation, so the reporter performed the Automatic Train Control and Positive Train Control (PTC) tests to make sure they were in compliance. PTC got stuck during the test, so the reporter tried troubleshooting by keying out and doing a hard reset. The PTC test finally passed, and the train was delayed seven minutes. Later, the reporter was informed the paperwork was okay to begin with.
